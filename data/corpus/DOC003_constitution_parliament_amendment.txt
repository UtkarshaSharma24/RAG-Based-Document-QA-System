The Constitution of India - Parliament and Amendment of the Constitution (simplified summary)

The Parliament of India consists of the President, the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States). Article 81 fixes the maximum strength of the Lok Sabha at 550 members. At present the Lok Sabha has 543 elected members. The normal term of the Lok Sabha is five years unless it is dissolved earlier.

The Rajya Sabha has a maximum strength of 250 members, of whom up to 12 are nominated by the President for their knowledge of literature, science, art and social service. The Rajya Sabha is a permanent house and is not dissolved. Each member serves a term of six years and one third of the members retire every two years.

Article 368 deals with the power of Parliament to amend the Constitution. Most amendments need a special majority, which means a majority of the total membership of each House and at least two thirds of the members present and voting. Amendments that affect the federal structure, such as the representation of states in Parliament, also need ratification by the legislatures of at least half of the states.

In the Kesavananda Bharati case of 1973, the Supreme Court held that Parliament can amend the Constitution but cannot change its basic structure. This is known as the basic structure doctrine.
