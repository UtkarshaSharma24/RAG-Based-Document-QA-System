The Constitution of India - Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties (simplified summary)

Part IV of the Constitution (Articles 36 to 51) contains the Directive Principles of State Policy. These principles guide the government in making laws and policies, for example securing adequate means of livelihood, equal pay for equal work for men and women, free legal aid, and protection of the environment. Article 37 says that Directive Principles are not enforceable by any court. This is the main difference between Directive Principles and Fundamental Rights, which can be enforced in court.

Fundamental Rights protect the individual against the State, whereas Directive Principles are goals that the State should try to achieve to build a welfare state. Courts have often read the two together so that they support each other.

Fundamental Duties are listed in Part IVA, Article 51A. They were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976 on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee. Originally there were ten duties. The eleventh duty was added by the 86th Amendment in 2002. It requires a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to a child between 6 and 14 years of age.

Some of the Fundamental Duties are to respect the Constitution, the National Flag and the National Anthem, to protect the sovereignty and integrity of India, to promote harmony among all people, to protect the environment, and to develop scientific temper. Fundamental Duties are not directly enforceable by courts.
