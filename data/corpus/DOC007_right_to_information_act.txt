Right to Information Act, 2005 - summary

The Right to Information (RTI) Act was passed in 2005 to promote transparency and accountability in the working of public authorities. It gives every citizen of India the right to request information held by a public authority. The Act applies to the Central Government, State Governments, and bodies that are substantially funded by the government.

To get information, a citizen files an application with the Public Information Officer (PIO) of the department. The applicant does not have to give a reason for asking for the information. The application fee for central government departments is 10 rupees, and people below the poverty line are exempted from the fee.

The PIO must give a reply within 30 days of receiving the application. If the information concerns the life or liberty of a person, it must be provided within 48 hours. If the applicant is not satisfied or does not get a reply, the applicant can file a first appeal with the First Appellate Authority in the same department, and then a second appeal with the Central Information Commission or the State Information Commission.

Section 8 of the Act lists exemptions, for example information that would harm the sovereignty and security of the country, information that is forbidden by a court, and personal information that has no relationship to any public activity. This is a general summary and not legal advice.
